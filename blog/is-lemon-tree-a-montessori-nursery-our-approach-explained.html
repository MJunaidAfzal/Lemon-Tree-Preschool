Is Lemon Tree a Montessori Nursery? Our Approach Explained

Yes. Lemon Tree runs on Montessori-style principles. A child picks their own activity from a shelf of purposefully chosen materials…

Yes. Lemon Tree runs on Montessori-style principles. A child picks their own activity from a shelf of purposefully chosen materials and stays with it until they’re done, rather than being walked through the same task as everyone else at the same time. If that’s the straight answer you came here for before booking a visit, you have it. What follows is how it actually plays out once a child walks through the door. Most parents searching this phrase aren’t after a dictionary definition; they want to know whether this fits how their own child learns.

What Sets This Approach Apart?

The short version: the child decides, the adult supports. A teacher doesn’t run the room from the front; each child works with materials that fit where they’re at, at their own pace, every day. Early numbers, practical stuff like threading a needle, bits of language work. The adult only steps in when asked, or when a child’s clearly ready for something harder.

It sounds small, but it changes a lot. How a teacher sees their own job. How a child feels walking in each morning. Even the shelves end up different, less about looking tidy, more about whether a three-year-old can actually reach what they need. Bit by bit, the room starts answering to the child, not the lesson plan.

A Typical Session at Lemon Tree

The Day Centres on Independent Work

Most of the session is built around children working through activities they’ve picked themselves. A staff member might crouch beside a child struggling with a task, offer a nudge, then step back; the goal is to let the child get there, not to finish it for them. Over time, this builds a habit of trying before asking, which tends to carry well beyond the nursery room itself.

Circle Time Gives the Day Its Shared Beat

A staff member might crouch down next to a child who is having a time with something. They might give a push or a gentle hint, then move back. The idea is not to do the task for the child, but to help them find their way. Slowly, this helps the child get used to trying instead of asking right away. That habit can stick. It doesn’t just stay in the nursery room. It stays. It follows the child into parts of life where trying before asking is useful.

Does “Montessori-Style” Mean Fully Certified?

Be careful with the assumption here, because the word “Montessori” has no legal protection in the UK; any setting can use it, whether they run a certified, traditional programme or simply borrow a few of its ideas. Lemon Tree falls into the second camp: it takes the core principles independence, choice, prepared materials and builds its own curriculum around them, all still delivered through the EYFS that every English nursery is required to follow. That distinction matters more than it might seem, since two nurseries using the same word on their website can run very different rooms.

If You’re Comparing Options in Manchester

The word on a website tells you little by itself. Nothing stops a setting from using it. What really makes a difference is how much of the day is truly in a child’s hands. It’s also about how staff respond the moment a child gets stuck. Whether the materials on the shelves are there to help build real independence or just to look good in photos. A short conversation with staff, during a visit, usually shows more than any page of marketing copy ever could.

Questions Worth Asking Before You Commit

A short list of direct questions usually tells you more in five minutes than a website will in five pages:

  • How much of a typical day is chosen by the child rather than led by staff?
  • What specific training have staff had in this approach?
  • What happens when a child can’t settle or focus on anything?
  • How does group time sit alongside the independent stretches?

Watch for confidence and specifics in the answers; vague responses are often the clearest sign of a setting that uses the label loosely. A good answer usually comes with an example, not just a reassurance.

The Bottom Line

Is Lemon Tree a Montessori nursery? Near enough in spirit, but not a strict, certified programme; it’s Montessori-style, with independence and self-paced choice sitting inside the EYFS framework. The only real way to know if it fits your child is to see a session for yourself and watch how they take to choosing their own path through it. A single visit, watched closely, usually tells you more than any amount of reading beforehand.
